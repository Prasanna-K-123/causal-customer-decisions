Change the retention assumptions

r is the probability of retaining a positive outcome divided by that of retaining a negative outcome, within an arm. Larger r means more positive enrichment. The true ratios are unknown.

p = s / [r × (1 − s) + s]

The allocation uses positive reconstructed net responses, capped at 20%. Means are standardized to the released test covariates. Original conditional exchangeability, a valid selection law and correct response models are additional assumptions. The original population’s covariate distribution is unknown.

Challenge the evidence

Why does covariate balance not settle identification?

A constant-X example has perfectly balanced covariates while outcome-dependent selection changes a true response difference of .02 into a released difference of .165. The exact oracle and tests are in the repository. Fitted treatment AUC near .5 cannot establish exchangeability of potential outcomes after selection.

What does the known-truth simulation show?

In a separate 60,000-training / 40,000-evaluation synthetic experiment, DR has the lowest PEHE among the fixed candidates. One of three pointwise policy intervals misses its known truth; that result is preserved. This is an implementation check, not a coverage study or evidence of Criteo effect identification.

What uncertainty is missing?

Intervals condition on trained models and the batch’s ranking cutoffs. Fingerprints are not verified person IDs, advertiser IDs are unavailable, and original-population selection is unidentified. The secondary conversion evaluation has only 51 positive control outcomes.

What would a next study change?

A new cohort, encoding, model budget, allocation objective or selection family needs a new protocol and a fresh evaluation design. The v1 test results cannot guide new confirmatory tuning. No deployment or original-campaign return is inferred here.
